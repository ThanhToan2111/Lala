# Abstract v1

Multimodal models often introduce increasingly complex fusion mechanisms without first establishing whether useful non-additive interaction is present in the representations being fused. We ask when multimodal interaction is measurable, relevant to a downstream task, faithfully represented, and accessible to a task learner. We develop a diagnostic hierarchy that separates cross-modal predictive identifiability, task joint headroom, interaction fidelity, and downstream accessibility. The central operational quantity is the joint predictive advantage of a capacity-controlled joint predictor over an additive predictor; its population counterpart is a squared-risk projection gap under explicit assumptions.

Controlled synthetic experiments and IPIB establish the behavior of the diagnostic, while Naturalized IPIB injects known interaction into frozen MOSEI vision/audio representation geometry. Across five seeds, increasing the injected interaction strength from β=0 to 1 increases validation joint predictive advantage from -0.01427 to 0.22625 and increases recovery of the known interaction component. On natural benchmarks, MOSEI exhibits a stable positive empirical advantage under the frozen protocol, but its distilled interaction does not improve sentiment accuracy over a lower-order baseline. MUStARD and MELD do not provide stable positive screens under the tested representations and hypothesis classes. A nested residual audit further finds no substantial additional MELD headroom.

These results show that predictive interaction, task relevance, representation fidelity, and accessibility can decouple. A preregistered B=50 alignment-breaking confirmation finds that the observed MOSEI advantage is not separated from this operational null (`p_hat=0.098`), reinforcing that the historical J=0.01 gate is operational rather than universally statistically calibrated. We conclude that multimodal interaction learning should be evidence-driven: non-additive structure must be measured and qualified before complex fusion mechanisms are interpreted as useful multimodal reasoning.
